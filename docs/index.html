The reference may be losing its buyers. On each of the 20 days to 11 November, buyers on OpenRouter, the venue where this price is measured, used more than twice as many tokens of DeepSeek V4.1-Flash-1017, the leading challenger, as of DeepSeek V4.1-Flash, the reference. Under section 4.5 of the methodology this is published as a note, and it changes nothing in the figure. If 1017 also leads the reference on qualifying providers on 20 census days in a row, the reference changes, after 30 assessment days in which both are published side by side with the ratio between them.

Why no price was published on 16 November. On Monday 16 November no price was published, and the cause was Hunterson's own, not any provider's. The account through which Hunterson buys on OpenRouter, the venue where this price is measured, had reached a monthly spending limit set on it, so OpenRouter refused every purchase in that day's window and no provider could complete a request. The limit was raised the same afternoon. Under section 7 of the methodology the day still counts against every provider's reliability, as does any window in which a provider completes no request. No published figure changes.

The price of AI inference, measured by buying it. Each weekday between 14:00 and 15:00 UTC, from London, Hunterson buys the same test job from every provider serving DeepSeek V4.1-Flash through one marketplace, and keeps the receipts. The figures are the averages of what the middle half of qualifying providers charged, per million input and per million output tokens. Standard covers models that use 30 billion active parameters or fewer for each token. A token is about three quarters of a word.
