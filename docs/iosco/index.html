Hunterson and the IOSCO principles

Statement of adherence, 30 October 2026. Reviewed each year, and updated as soon as anything in it changes; every version is kept in this repository's history.

Hunterson Standard Inference, Europe is written with reference to the IOSCO Principles for Oil Price Reporting Agencies, published by the International Organization of Securities Commissions on 5 October 2012 (FR06/12). They are the principles written for agencies that assess the price of a physical product, which is what Hunterson does: it buys a defined quantity of AI model output each weekday and publishes what it cost. IOSCO's Principles for Financial Benchmarks of 2013 are written for figures built from data that others submit; Hunterson's figures are built from its own purchases. Which set applies is looked at again when the first use of the figures in a contract or financial product is registered under the data licence.

This statement takes each paragraph of the principles in turn and says whether Hunterson meets it, meets it in another form, does not need to because the paragraph does not apply, or does not meet it. The principles' own numbering is kept; their text has no paragraph 2.13.

In short. Hunterson is produced by one person. Four principles need a second person, a board or an independent auditor, and are not met as written: supervision and sign-off before release (2.8), segregation of functions and reporting lines (2.12(c) and 2.15), independent handling of complaints with recourse to a third party (2.18(d) and (g)), and an annual external audit (2.21). What stands in their place is said against each. Several principles are met more strictly than the principles require, above all the hierarchy of data (2.2(b)): every observation is a completed purchase with its receipt.

What would change this statement

Any of the following would be recorded here at once: a second person taking part in the production or the review of complaints; a named independent reviewer for complaints; an external audit; a change in the venue or in how the data is obtained; or a decision that IOSCO's Principles for Financial Benchmarks should apply instead.
